Claude can build a sales page, a lead quiz, a lead tracker, a revenue calendar, SOPs, automation maps, proposals, a numbers review and a business assistant from plain-language prompts. Each build below replaces a paid tool or a manual task for a coach, consultant or service founder.

Who this is for

Coaches, consultants and service founders who run the business themselves.

What you need

A Claude account, your offer details and about 20 minutes per build.

Before you start

Write your brief once

Every build works better when Claude knows your business. You write the brief one time and paste it at the top of each new chat.

  1. Open Claude

    Sign in or create a free account.

    claude.aiOpen
  2. Fill in the business brief
    1. Copy the brief below into a note.
    2. Replace every bracket with one line about your business.
    3. Keep the note open so you can paste it into each build.
  3. Gather what a few builds need
    1. Your brand hex codes and font names for items 01, 02 and 07.
    2. A screen recording of one repeated task for item 05.
    3. Your last month of Instagram and Stripe numbers for item 08.
  4. Use one chat per build

    Start a fresh chat for each item and paste the brief first.

Business brief
Here is my business brief. Use it for this whole chat.
Business: [what you sell, in one sentence]
Who it is for: [your ideal client and their main problem]
Offer and price: [offer name, price, what is included]
Voice: [a few words, e.g. direct, warm, plain]
Brand: [colors as hex codes, fonts you use]
Goal this quarter: [revenue number]
Reply with "ready" and nothing else.
Note

Replace every bracket before you send. When a bracket stays empty, Claude fills the gap with a guess.

01

Sales pages

Your offer, finally clear.

Replaces Leadpages and a $2,000 designer

Steps

  1. Open a fresh Claude chat

    Paste your business brief first so Claude knows your offer and voice.

  2. Paste the sales page prompt

    Fill every bracket with your real details.

    1. Replace each bracket with your offer, price and ideal client.
    2. Add one or two client results if you have them.
    3. Send the prompt.
  3. Read the first draft

    Check it as a buyer would.

    1. Read the headline out loud. It should name the client and the result.
    2. Confirm the booking button shows near the top and again at the bottom.
  4. Ask for changes in plain language

    Examples: "Shorten the headline." or "Add an FAQ about payment plans."

  5. Publish the page

    Get the file out of Claude and onto the web.

    1. Ask Claude: "Give me this as one HTML file I can host."
    2. Upload the file to a free static host such as GitHub Pages, Netlify or Cloudflare Pages, or share it as a Claude artifact link.
    3. Open the live link on your phone and click the booking button.

Prompt to copy

Prompt
Build a one-page sales page for my offer as a single HTML file.
Offer: [offer name and what the client gets]
Price: [price]
Who it is for: [ideal client and their main problem]
Sections: headline, problem, offer, what is included,
proof, FAQ, booking button.
Style: my brand colors and fonts from the brief. Mobile first.
Write short, plain sentences. No hype words.
The button says "Book a Call" and links to [your calendar link].
Tip

Your traffic arrives from Instagram, so check the page on your phone before you share it.

You end up with One HTML sales page with a booking button, ready to host.

02

Lead magnets

What's leaking in your funnel?

Replaces Another PDF nobody opens

Steps

  1. Choose one problem your client has

    Write it as a first-person sentence, such as "My content isn't reaching the right people."

  2. Paste the scorecard prompt

    Claude builds the quiz and the scoring.

    1. Fill in the title and the audience.
    2. Name the next step each result should point to.
  3. Check the scoring

    A quiz with broken math loses trust fast.

    1. Ten questions at 10 points each add up to 100. Confirm the top score reads 100.
    2. Read the three results and confirm each score band gives different advice.
  4. Add email capture

    Ask Claude: "Ask for name and email before the result shows, and tell me how to connect it to my email tool."

  5. Publish and link it

    Use the same hosting route as item 01, then put the link in your bio and your DM automation.

Prompt to copy

Prompt
Build an interactive scorecard quiz as a single HTML file.
Title: "What's leaking in your [funnel or business]?"
Audience: [ideal client]
Write 10 questions. Each has 3 answers worth 0, 5 or 10 points.
Show a score out of 100 with a progress bar at the end.
Write a short result for each score band: low, mid, high.
Each result ends with a button to [your next step].
Use my brand colors. Mobile first.

You end up with A scorecard quiz people finish, with a result and a next step.

03

Your CRM

A live tracker that does the math for you.

Replaces A $50 a month tool

Steps

  1. Paste the tracker prompt

    Use a new chat and paste your business brief first.

  2. Add your first leads

    Test it with real data.

    1. Type in three current leads with their stage and deal value.
    2. Confirm close rate, show rate and pipeline value update on screen.
  3. Test the math

    Break it on purpose.

    1. Mark one lead as signed and watch close rate change.
    2. Move one lead to lost and watch pipeline value drop.
  4. Add a backup

    Ask Claude: "Add an Export CSV button and an Import CSV button."

  5. Update it every Friday

    Move each lead to its current stage. It takes five minutes.

Prompt to copy

Prompt
Build a lead tracker as a single HTML file that saves in my browser.
Columns: lead name, stage, deal value, date added.
Stages: new, nurturing, call booked, showed, signed, lost.
Let me add, edit and delete leads.
At the top, calculate and show:
- close rate: signed divided by calls showed
- show rate: calls showed divided by calls booked
- pipeline value: total value of open deals
Color-code the stage labels. Keep it clean and fast.
Warning

A tracker that saves in your browser keeps data on that one device. Add the CSV export and download a backup each week. When you need email sync or a team inside it, move to a full CRM.

You end up with A lead tracker that shows close rate, show rate and pipeline value.

04

Revenue calendar

Your revenue goal, reverse-engineered into a weekly calendar.

Replaces Guessing how many DMs and calls you need

Steps

  1. Write down two numbers

    Your monthly revenue goal and your offer price.

  2. Work out the clients you need

    Divide the goal by the price. Example: $20,000 divided by $4,000 is 5 clients.

  3. Paste the calendar prompt

    Claude works backwards to DMs, calls and follow-ups.

    1. Fill in your goal and price.
    2. Add your real rates if you know them. If you do not, leave them out and Claude will label its numbers as estimates.
  4. Read the math

    Every number should trace back to your goal.

    1. Check that each day's job connects to the clients you need.
    2. Swap the estimates for your own rates after your first month.
  5. Block the week

    Put each day's job in your calendar as a recurring block.

Prompt to copy

Prompt
Reverse-engineer my revenue goal into a weekly plan.
Monthly goal: [$ amount]
Offer price: [$ amount]
Clients needed: the goal divided by the price.
My rates: [DM to reply %], [reply to call %], [call to client %]
If I do not know a rate, use a fair estimate and label it.
Work backwards to the DMs, calls and follow-ups I need each week.
Then give me a Monday to Friday calendar with one job per day.
Include one batch content day. Show the math.

You end up with A Monday to Friday plan built backwards from your revenue goal.

05

Instant SOPs

Record yourself doing it once, get a clean SOP back.

Replaces Explaining the same job from scratch every time

Steps

  1. Pick one task you repeat

    Example: onboarding a new client.

  2. Record it once

    Talk through each step as you do it.

    1. Open Loom or any screen recorder.
    2. Do the task and say each step out loud.
    3. Stop the recording and copy the transcript.
  3. Paste the SOP prompt with your transcript

    Add the task name and who will do it.

  4. Answer the flagged steps

    Claude asks about anything the transcript left unclear. Reply to each one.

  5. Save the SOP where your hire can open it

    A shared doc works. Add the date at the top.

Prompt to copy

Prompt
Turn this transcript into a clean SOP.
Task name: [task]
Who will do it: [role]
Format: purpose, tools needed, numbered steps,
and a short "if something breaks" list.
One action per step. Start each step with a verb.
If any part of the transcript is unclear, ask me before you write that step.
Transcript:
[paste transcript here]

You end up with A written SOP your first hire can follow without you.

06

Automations

Your DM and Zapier automations, mapped step by step.

Replaces Piecing the logic together inside the tool

Steps

  1. Choose your trigger keyword

    Example: a comment of "FELT" on a post.

  2. Paste the automation prompt

    Name the tool you will build it in.

  3. Review every message

    Read each DM as your client would.

    1. Cut anything that sounds like a sales script.
    2. Keep each message to two short sentences.
  4. Build it in your tool

    Claude writes the logic and you click it in.

    1. Create one step in your tool for each numbered line.
    2. Paste the exact message text into each step.
    3. Add the fallback for people who do not reply.
  5. Test with a second account

    Comment the keyword and walk through every step yourself.

Prompt to copy

Prompt
Map a DM automation for me, step by step.
Trigger: someone comments "[keyword]" on my post.
Goal: [what they should end up doing, e.g. book a call]
Tool I will build it in: [ManyChat, Zapier, GoHighLevel or other]
For each step give me:
- the trigger or action
- the exact message text
- the condition that moves them to the next step
Add a fallback for people who do not reply in 24 hours.
End with the full flow as a numbered list I can click in.
Note

Claude writes the flow and the message text. It does not connect to your Instagram account, so you build the flow inside your automation tool.

You end up with A numbered flow with the exact messages, ready to build in your tool.

Proposals

Proposals and contracts in your colors, customized per client.

Replaces A HoneyBook subscription

Steps

  1. Collect your brand details

    Hex codes for your colors and the names of your fonts.

  2. Paste the proposal prompt

    Fill in one real client as the first example.

  3. Review the template

    Fill it once for a real client.

    1. Confirm the client-specific fields stand out so you can swap them fast.
    2. Read the proposal from cover to signature line.
  4. Save it as a PDF

    Use your browser.

    1. Open the HTML file in your browser and choose Print.
    2. Set the destination to Save as PDF.
  5. Send it with your payment link

    Use the payment tool you already have.

Prompt to copy

Prompt
Create a proposal template as a single HTML file I can save as a PDF.
Brand: my colors and fonts from the brief.
Client name: [client]
Project: [what I will deliver]
Investment: [price and payment terms]
Sections: cover, the problem, my approach, scope, timeline,
investment, terms, signature line.
Keep the copy tight. One page per section at most.
Mark every client-specific field so I can swap it for the next client.
Important

Have a lawyer review your contract terms before you use them with clients. Claude drafts the wording, and the legal check is yours to do.

You end up with A branded proposal template you can swap per client and save as a PDF.

08

Your numbers

Drop in your Instagram or Stripe numbers and ask what's working and what to stop.

Replaces Hours of staring at dashboards

Steps

  1. Collect this month's numbers

    Keep it to the figures you already track.

    1. Instagram: reach, saves and DMs.
    2. Stripe: revenue and new clients.
    3. The topics of your top five posts.
  2. Paste the numbers prompt

    Fill in each bracket from your exports.

  3. Ask one follow-up

    Example: "Which post topic brought the most DMs?"

  4. Write the decision

    Finish with one line that starts with Do more and one that starts with Stop.

Prompt to copy

Prompt
Here are my numbers for [month].
Instagram: reach [ ], saves [ ], DMs [ ]
Stripe: revenue [ ], new clients [ ]
Top posts and their topics: [paste]
Tell me what is working and what to stop.
Back each point with the numbers I gave you.
Keep it to one screen.
If a point is a guess, say so.
Tip

Claude reasons from the numbers you paste. Add the topic of each top post so it can connect results to content.

You end up with A short do-more and stop list based on your own numbers.

09

Your assistant

One assistant loaded with your whole business.

Replaces Re-explaining yourself in every chat

Steps

  1. Create a Project in Claude

    Projects keep one set of context for every chat inside them.

    1. Open Projects in the Claude sidebar.
    2. Create a new project and name it after your business.
  2. Add your project knowledge

    Save each one as a short file and add it to the project.

    1. offer.md with your offer and what is included.
    2. icp.md with your ideal client.
    3. voice.md with three samples of your own writing.
    4. pricing.md and numbers.md with current figures.
  3. Paste the instructions

    Put the prompt below into the project instructions.

  4. Test it with a real request

    Example: "Write my hooks for the week."

  5. Refresh it monthly

    Update numbers.md each month so decisions use current figures.

Project instructions to copy

Project instructions
You are my business assistant. Always use the project files.
Offer: see offer.md
Ideal client: see icp.md
Voice: see voice.md. Match it and never copy phrases.
Pricing: see pricing.md
Numbers: see numbers.md. I update it monthly.
When I ask for content, write it in my voice.
When I ask for a decision, use my numbers and state what you assumed.
If something is missing, ask me one question.

You end up with A Claude Project that already knows your offer, voice, pricing and numbers.

FAQ

Questions answered

Can Claude really replace these paid tools?

For a solo founder or a small team, Claude builds a working first version of each one from your prompt, and you own the file. A paid tool still wins when you need email sync, team permissions or a page that handles heavy traffic.

Is Claude free to use for this?

These prompts run in a normal Claude chat, and Claude has a free plan. Message limits and some features depend on your plan. If you hit a cap, wait for it to reset or move to a paid plan for heavier use.

Where do I host the sales page for free?

Ask Claude for a single HTML file. Upload it to a free static host such as GitHub Pages, Netlify or Cloudflare Pages, or share it as a Claude artifact link. You can connect your own domain later.

Will my tracker keep my leads?

A tracker that saves in the browser keeps data on that one device and browser. Ask Claude to add an Export CSV button, download a backup each week, and move to a full CRM once your pipeline outgrows one screen.

Can Claude run my DM automations?

Claude writes the flow and the exact messages. You then build it in your automation tool, such as ManyChat or Zapier, by following the steps. Claude does not connect to your Instagram account on its own.

How do I stop re-explaining my business in every chat?

Create a Claude Project, add your offer, ideal client, voice, pricing and numbers as project files, and write instructions that point to them. Every chat inside that project starts with that context. Item 09 has the full setup.
