Standard Operating Procedure

Run Fable 5 like a system, not a chatbot.

The most capable model Anthropic has shipped runs on different rules than the one you got used to. This procedure covers the six operating shifts that decide whether Fable 5 works for you or just sounds impressive.

Fable 5 is built to take a job that would normally eat your whole week and finish it start to finish, on its own, correct the first time. The habits that made older models behave now hold this one back. This document is the repeatable procedure for getting that first-shot result every time you open the model.

Before you start

Who this is for, and what you need

Who this is for

  • Solo founders running lean, without a team to hand work to
  • Coaches and creators who write, build and ship their own assets
  • Service businesses that want a real deliverable, not a first draft to fix

Tools you need

  • Access to Fable 5 through the Claude app or Claude Platform
  • The effort setting exposed in your interface
  • The files, context or connectors the job needs
  • One real task worth finishing, not a test question
Note

Work through the shifts in order the first few times. Once they are habit, you will reach for them automatically and the frame at the end becomes your default opening.

01

Give it a goal, not a list of steps

Breaking a task into tiny numbered steps used to help. On Fable 5 it backfires, because you end up boxing in the exact reasoning you wanted from it. Do this instead:

  1. State the goal in one plain sentence.
  2. Describe what a finished, good result contains, so the model knows what done looks like.
  3. Stop typing and let it choose its own path to get there.
Try this
I need a client onboarding email sequence that gets replies. Done means five emails, in my voice, ready to load into my CRM.
02

Match the effort dial to the job

There is a setting for how hard the model works, and it usually goes untouched. Matching that setting to the size of the job is the single biggest lever you have.

EffortUse it for
LowQuick drafts, brainstorming, throwaway text where speed beats depth
MediumEveryday tasks and standard writing that need to be solid, not exhaustive
HighComplex builds, deep research and multi-step work. The right default for anything that matters
Tip

Default to High and drop it down when you want speed, rather than the reverse. Reaching for depth after a shallow answer costs you a second pass.

03

Give it tools, not just instructions

When Fable 5 can run tests, read your files or search, it checks its own work before it hands it back. That self-verification is where the first-shot accuracy comes from. So don't script every step:

  1. Hand over the files, data or context the job depends on.
  2. Turn on the connectors or tools it needs to check its own output.
  3. Brief it like a senior hire you trust, then let it verify itself.
04

Set boundaries before it acts

More capability means it will move without being asked. It may draft the email, create a backup branch, or expand the scope you gave it. Useful, until it isn't. So be explicit about when to check with you and when to just go.

Try this
When I'm describing a problem or thinking out loud, give me your assessment and stop. Don't make changes until I ask. Pause before anything you can't undo.
Warning

Set boundaries before you hand over anything irreversible, such as sending, publishing or overwriting. Add the boundary line up front, not after it has already acted.

05

Give it the reason, not just the request

It performs better when it knows why you're asking, because the context lets it connect your task to everything around it instead of guessing at your intent. One line does it.

Try this
I'm building X for Y. They need it to do Z. With that in mind, here's what I want.
06

Start at the top of your difficulty range

The instinct is to test a new model on easy work, which never shows you what it can do. Point it at the hard thing instead:

  1. Hand Fable 5 the difficult task you have been avoiding.
  2. Ask it to scope the task and tell you what it needs from you.
  3. Give it those inputs and let it run the whole thing.

That is where it earns its keep, and where you find out what to stop doing by hand.

Reusable template

The starter frame, steal this

Every shift above collapses into one opening you can reuse. Paste it, fill the brackets, and send.

Prompt Goal: [the outcome you want, in one plain sentence] Done looks like: [what a finished, good result contains] Context: I'm building this for [who] so they can [what it enables]. Effort: high. Boundaries: assess first, don't change anything until I say go. Over to you. Ask me anything you need before you start.
Important

Come back to this frame the next time you open Fable 5 and catch yourself typing step one of ten. The habit is the whole point.

If it goes sideways

Troubleshooting

SymptomLikely causeFix
Output is shallow or generic Effort left on Low, or no reason given Raise effort to High and add the one-line context of who it is for and why
It followed your steps but missed the point You scripted the path instead of the goal Restate the outcome and what done looks like, then let it plan
It changed or sent something you did not approve No boundaries set up front Add the assess-first boundary line at the start of the brief
Answers feel confident but wrong No tools to check its own work Give it the files, data or search it needs to verify before answering
