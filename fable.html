The Fable 5.2 setup: 3 things to make before you pay

You have a short window with Claude Fable 5.2. Use it to build three things that keep working long after the window closes. Every step has a prompt you can copy and paste. Do them in order, because each part builds on the one before.

#You buildWhat it is
1A chief of staffAn AI that knows you and runs your week
2A plan for your whole 2027Built from the hard questions, broken down to this week
3An Opus cheat sheetSo you keep the same quality after you start paying
Time-sensitive

This guide assumes Fable 5.2 is available on your plan for a limited time. Check your own plan to see what you can use and until when.

Before you start (10 minutes)

What you need

  • Claude, with Fable 5.2 selected as the model.
  • About 60 to 90 minutes per day for 3 days.
  • A notes app or a folder to save four files. You create them as you go.
FileWhat it holdsMade in
ABOUT-ME.mdWho you are, how you work, what you wantPart 1
OPERATING-SYSTEM.mdGoals, morning brief, weekly review, call-out rulesPart 1
PLAN-2027.mdYour year, broken down to this weekPart 2
OPUS-CHEAT-SHEET.mdHow Fable thinks, written for Opus to followPart 3

Set up a home for it

Create a Project in Claude called Chief of Staff (or any place where you can save custom instructions and files that every chat can see). Everything below happens inside that project. As you finish each file, add it to the project's knowledge or files so every future chat can read it.

Three rules for the best results

  1. Answer honestly, not impressively. The output is only as good as your answers.
  2. Use voice typing for the interview if you can. You will say more than you would type.
  3. Do not paste anything you would not want stored: passwords, account numbers, other people's private details.

Part 1

Make Fable your chief of staff

Most people use AI like an assistant that waits to be asked. A chief of staff works the other way: it learns how you think, then tells you what matters today.

Step 1.1

Let it interview you

Start a new chat in your project and paste:

Prompt
You are going to become my chief of staff. Before you can do that, you need to know me better than my friends do. Interview me.

How to run the interview:
- Ask ONE question at a time. Wait for my answer before asking the next.
- Cover these areas, in roughly this order:
  1. My work: what I do, who I serve, how I make money, what a great week looks like.
  2. My goals: this year, the next 3 years, and what I secretly want but rarely say out loud.
  3. How I work: my energy across the day, when I do my best thinking, what derails me, how I procrastinate.
  4. My habits and routines: mornings, evenings, health, sleep, phone use.
  5. My people: who matters most, who drains me, where I need support.
  6. My blind spots: patterns I repeat, excuses I use, what people who know me well would say I avoid.
  7. How I want to be held accountable: how direct you should be, what motivates me, what makes me shut down.
- Ask follow-up questions whenever my answer is vague, short, or contradicts something I said earlier. Do not accept "I don't know" the first time; rephrase the question.
- After every 10 questions, give me a 3-line summary of what you have learned so far and ask if it is accurate.
- Ask between 30 and 40 questions in total. Stop early only if I type "DONE".
- Be warm but direct. No flattery. Do not give advice yet; only learn.

Start with your first question.
Tip

Block out 30 to 45 minutes. The best material usually comes after question 20.

Step 1.2

Turn the interview into your profile

When the interview ends, paste this in the same chat:

Prompt
Using everything from this interview, write my profile as a markdown file called ABOUT-ME.md.

Use exactly these sections:
1. Snapshot: who I am in 5 sentences.
2. Work: what I do, who I serve, how I make money, current priorities.
3. Goals: this year, 3 years, and the unspoken goal.
4. How I work best: energy pattern, best hours, ideal week shape.
5. What derails me: my top procrastination patterns and their triggers.
6. Blind spots: patterns I repeat, with a quote from my own answers as evidence for each.
7. People: who to protect time for, who drains me.
8. How to hold me accountable: tone, directness level, what works, what backfires.
9. Open questions: things you still do not know about me and would ask next.

Rules:
- Use my own words wherever possible.
- Be specific. "Works best 6 to 10 am, crashes after lunch" beats "is a morning person".
- Do not soften the blind spots.
Save it

Copy the result into ABOUT-ME.md and add it to your project files. Read it once. If anything is wrong, tell Fable and ask it to fix that section.

Step 1.3

Build your operating system

Start a new chat in the project (so it reads ABOUT-ME.md fresh) and paste:

Prompt
Read ABOUT-ME.md. You are now my chief of staff. Build my personal operating system as a markdown file called OPERATING-SYSTEM.md with these four parts.

PART A: GOALS, DOWN TO THE NEXT SMALL STEP
- List my 3 most important goals for the next 12 months (from my profile; ask me if unclear).
- Break each goal into: 3 to 4 quarterly milestones, then this month's target, then this week's focus, then ONE next small step I can finish in under 30 minutes today.
- Format each goal as a tree so I can see the path from the big goal to the next step.

PART B: MORNING BRIEF
Design a morning brief I will ask you for every day. It must take under 3 minutes to read and include:
- Today's ONE priority, and why it matters to which goal.
- The next small step for each goal (one line each).
- My calendar or commitments today (I will paste them, or say "none").
- One pattern from my blind spots to watch for today.
- One question to think about.
Write the exact template and the exact prompt I will use to ask for it.

PART C: WEEKLY REVIEW
Design a 20-minute weekly review we will do every Sunday:
- What I said I would do vs what I actually did. Score each goal 0 to 10.
- What got in the way, named honestly.
- What to stop, start, and keep.
- Next week's focus and next small steps.
- Updates to my goals or this operating system.
Write the exact questions you will ask me, in order, and the prompt I will use to start it.

PART D: CALL-OUT RULES
Write the rules for when and how you call me out. Include:
- What counts as a slip (be specific to my patterns, e.g. "skipped the next step 2 days in a row", "planned instead of shipping").
- What you do at the first slip, the second, and the third.
- The tone to use, based on ABOUT-ME.md section 8.
- The exact phrases you will use. Example: "You said Friday. It is Saturday. What happened, and what is the new date?"
- One rule that you will never let me break, chosen from my blind spots.

End with a "How to use this system" section: what I say each morning, each Sunday, and when I slip.
Save it

Copy the result into OPERATING-SYSTEM.md and add it to your project files.

Step 1.4

Make it keep running

1. Put the rules in the project instructions

Paste this into your project's custom instructions:

Project instructions
You are my chief of staff. Always read ABOUT-ME.md and OPERATING-SYSTEM.md before responding (and PLAN-2027.md once it exists).
- When I say "brief", give me today's morning brief using the template in OPERATING-SYSTEM.md.
- When I say "review", run the weekly review, asking one question at a time.
- When I say "slip", or when you notice me avoiding my next step, apply the call-out rules.
- When I mention a new commitment, ask which goal it serves. If none, say so.
- Keep answers short unless I ask for detail. Be direct. No flattery.
- When something important changes about me or my goals, suggest an exact edit to the right file.

2. Use the three daily words

  • Every morning: open a chat in the project and type brief. Paste today's calendar if you have one.
  • Every Sunday: type review.
  • Whenever you drift: type slip, or let it catch you.

3. Optional: automate it

If your Claude plan offers scheduled tasks, schedule brief for every weekday morning and review for Sunday evening. If not, set two phone reminders that say "brief" and "review".

Check it worked

Tomorrow's brief should name ONE priority and a next step that fits your real day. If it feels generic, tell it what was off and ask it to update OPERATING-SYSTEM.md.

Part 2

Let Fable fix your life

The people around you are too polite to ask the hard questions. Fable is not.

Step 2.1

The hard questions

New chat in the project. Paste:

Prompt
Read ABOUT-ME.md and OPERATING-SYSTEM.md. Now run a life audit with me. Ask the hard questions nobody around me will ask.

How to run it:
- ONE question at a time. Wait for my answer.
- Cover all 8 areas: work and money, health and energy, relationships, family, friendships, personal growth, fun and rest, and meaning (what I want my life to be about).
- For each area ask at least 2 questions, and make at least one of them uncomfortable. Examples of the level I want:
  - "What are you avoiding, and what is it costing you each month?"
  - "If you kept living exactly like this for 5 more years, what would you regret most?"
  - "What would you quit tomorrow if nobody would judge you?"
  - "Who do you want to be in a year, and what does that person do every day that you do not?"
  - "Where are you telling yourself a story to avoid a decision?"
- Push back when my answer sounds like an excuse, and say so gently but clearly.
- Use what you know from my profile to make the questions personal, not generic.
- After all 8 areas, give each a score from 1 to 10 based on my answers, with one sentence of reasoning, and ask me if I agree.

Start with your first question.
Tip

This one can feel uncomfortable. That is the point. Answer for yourself, not for an audience.

Step 2.2

Turn your answers into a plan for 2027

Same chat. Paste:

Prompt
Turn this audit into my plan for 2027. Write it as a markdown file called PLAN-2027.md.

Structure:
1. The theme of my 2027 in one sentence, plus 3 words for who I am becoming.
2. What I am STOPPING in 2027 (at least 3 things, taken from my answers).
3. My 3 to 5 big outcomes for the year. Each one measurable, with a deadline, linked to the life area it fixes.
4. Quarter by quarter: for Q1 to Q4, the milestone for each outcome and the one habit to build that quarter.
5. Month by month for Q1 only: the target for each month.
6. My first 7 days: one concrete action per day, each under 30 minutes.
7. The 3 biggest risks to this plan, based on my blind spots, and exactly what I will do when each one shows up.
8. A one-paragraph letter from December 2027 me to today's me.

Rules:
- Make it realistic for my actual energy, time and money, as described in my profile. A smaller plan I will follow beats a big plan I will abandon.
- Use my words from the audit.
- No vague goals like "be healthier". Say what, how much, and by when.
Save it

Copy the result into PLAN-2027.md and add it to your project files.

Step 2.3

Connect the plan to your operating system

New chat in the project. Paste:

Prompt
Read PLAN-2027.md and OPERATING-SYSTEM.md. Update OPERATING-SYSTEM.md so the goals in Part A come from my 2027 plan, the next small steps match my first 7 days, and the call-out rules cover the 3 risks in the plan. Show me the full updated file.

Replace OPERATING-SYSTEM.md in your project with the new version. From now on, your morning brief is running your 2027 plan.

Part 3

Keep the results after you start paying

When the free window ends, you may switch to Opus for everyday work. The risk is losing the way Fable thinks about you. The fix is a cheat sheet: Fable writes down how it thinks, and Opus follows it every time.

Step 3.1

Have Fable write how it thinks

New chat in the project, still using Fable 5.2. Paste:

Prompt
Read all my project files. I am going to switch to Claude Opus for daily use. I want Opus to work with me exactly the way you do. Write OPUS-CHEAT-SHEET.md: instructions written FOR Opus, in the second person ("You..."), that capture how you think about me and my work.

Include these sections:
1. Who I am, in 10 bullet points. Only what changes how you should respond.
2. How you think through my requests: the actual steps you take, in order, before answering.
3. Decision rules: when I ask for X, you do Y. Give at least 10, specific to me. Example: "When I ask for a plan, always end with one next step under 30 minutes."
4. My goals and current priorities, and how to check any request against them.
5. Tone and format: length, directness, structure, words to use and to avoid.
6. Accountability: the call-out rules, written so another model can apply them exactly.
7. The morning brief and weekly review: the exact templates and how to run them.
8. Do and don't: 10 of each, specific to me.
9. Three worked examples: a realistic request from me, then the ideal response, in full. Choose examples that show your judgment, not easy cases.
10. Self-check: 5 questions Opus should ask itself before every answer.

Rules:
- Be concrete. Every rule should be something Opus can apply without guessing.
- Do not include generic advice that would be true for anyone.
- Keep it under 2,500 words so it fits comfortably in any instructions field.
Save it

Copy the result into OPUS-CHEAT-SHEET.md.

Step 3.2

Test it before the window closes

This is the step most people skip. Do not skip it.

  1. Pick 3 real tasks you would normally bring to your chief of staff, for example: "brief", "help me decide whether to take this client", "I skipped my workout twice this week".
  2. Ask Fable each one in your project. Save the answers.
  3. Start a new chat, switch the model to Opus, paste the contents of OPUS-CHEAT-SHEET.md first, then ask the same 3 tasks.
  4. Compare the answers side by side.

Then go back to Fable and paste:

Prompt
Here are your answers and Opus's answers to the same 3 tasks, using the cheat sheet.

[paste Fable's answer 1] / [paste Opus's answer 1]
[paste Fable's answer 2] / [paste Opus's answer 2]
[paste Fable's answer 3] / [paste Opus's answer 3]

Where did Opus fall short of how you would answer? For each gap, name the missing rule, then give me an updated OPUS-CHEAT-SHEET.md that fixes it. Keep it under 2,500 words.

Repeat once or twice until the Opus answers feel like Fable's.

Step 3.3

Install it for good

  • Make a second project called Chief of Staff (Opus), or switch the model in your existing project.
  • Paste OPUS-CHEAT-SHEET.md at the top of the project instructions, followed by the instructions from Step 1.4.
  • Add all four files to the project files.
  • Keep using brief, review and slip exactly as before.

Once a month, ask Opus:

Monthly prompt
Based on this month's chats, what should change in OPUS-CHEAT-SHEET.md, ABOUT-ME.md or PLAN-2027.md?

Apply the edits and your system keeps getting smarter.

Troubleshooting

If something goes wrong

ProblemFix
The answers feel genericYour files are too thin. Run the interview again for 10 more questions on the weak area, then regenerate that file
It is too soft (or too harsh)Tell it, then ask it to update section 8 of ABOUT-ME.md and Part D of OPERATING-SYSTEM.md
You stopped using it after a weekShrink the morning brief to 3 lines and make brief the first thing you type every day. Consistency beats depth
The plan feels overwhelmingAsk: "Cut PLAN-2027.md in half. Keep only what moves the biggest outcome."
Opus does not sound like FableRepeat Step 3.2 with harder test tasks. The worked examples in section 9 matter most

  • PLAN-2027.md saved and connected to the operating system
  • OPUS-CHEAT-SHEET.md saved
  • Tested with 3 real tasks and refined
  • Opus project set up with all four files
