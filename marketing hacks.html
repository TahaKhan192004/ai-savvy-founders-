Before you start

How every hack works

A Claude hack is a saved prompt plus a connection to one of your apps. Claude reads the data, finds the pattern and hands you a short report or a draft. You stay in charge of what gets sent.

  • Connect the app once. In Claude, open Settings then Connectors and sign in to each tool the hack uses.
  • Run the prompt by hand first. Check the output looks right before you automate it.
  • Then schedule it. Ask Claude to turn the prompt into a scheduled task on the day and time you want.
  • Fill in the highlighted parts. Text shown like this inside a prompt is where your own details go.

01

A chief of staff before you open your laptop

Before you're up, Claude checks four apps and hands you one page. You start the day knowing where everything stands, without opening four tabs to find out.

Google Calendar Gmail Asana Slack

What you get each morning

  • Emails that need you today, each with a draft reply ready to send
  • Today's calls, with a line on who each person is and what was last said
  • Asana tasks that are overdue or stuck
  • Slack threads where your team is waiting on you

Set it up

  1. Open Settings then Connectors in Claude and connect Gmail, Google Calendar, Asana and Slack.
  2. Start a new chat, make sure all four connectors are switched on, and paste the prompt below.
  3. Read the brief. If a section is noisy, add a line to the prompt telling Claude what to skip.
  4. Reply with: Run this every weekday at 6:30am as a scheduled task.
Prompt
You are my chief of staff. Check my Gmail, Google Calendar, Asana and Slack and give me a one-page brief for today. 1. EMAILS: Unread or starred emails from the last 24 hours that need a reply from me. Skip newsletters, receipts and notifications. For each: sender, one-line summary, and a short draft reply in my voice (direct, warm, no fluff). Do not send anything. 2. CALLS: Every meeting on my calendar today. For each: time, who it's with, one line on who they are, and one line on what we last discussed (search Gmail and Slack for our most recent exchange). 3. ASANA: Tasks that are overdue or haven't moved in 5+ days. Show the task, the owner and how many days late. 4. SLACK: Threads and DMs where someone tagged me or asked me a question and I haven't replied. Show who, the channel and what they need. Finish with my top priorities for today, in order. Keep the whole brief to one page.

Tip

Add your role and two lines about your current priorities to the top of the prompt. Claude ranks the brief far better when it knows what matters this quarter.
02

Find out why you're actually losing deals

Claude reads your last 20 sales calls and shows you the objection that keeps coming up on the calls that didn't close. No more guessing. The pattern comes from your own calls.

Fathom

What you get

  • The objections from lost calls, ranked by how often they came up
  • What you did differently on the calls that closed
  • Your buyers' exact words for their problem, ready to paste into your sales page

Set it up

  1. Connect Fathom from the Connectors directory in Claude. If you can't find it there, export your last 20 call transcripts from Fathom and upload them to the chat instead.
  2. Write down which of those calls closed. Claude can't see deal outcomes unless you tell it.
  3. Paste the prompt below with your list of closed deals filled in.
  4. Repeat this once a month and compare the reports.
Prompt
Pull my last 20 sales calls from Fathom. These calls closed: names of the prospects who bought Treat every other call as not closed. 1. List every objection raised on the calls that didn't close. Rank them by how often they came up and quote the line where each one was said, with the call name. 2. Compare with the calls that closed. When the same objection came up there, what did I say or do differently? 3. Pull the exact phrases buyers used to describe their problem, word for word. Group them by theme and note how many calls each theme appeared on. 4. Write 5 headline or bullet lines for my sales page using only the buyers' own words. Be blunt. If the problem is how I run the call, say so.
03

Never walk into a sales call cold

When someone books a call, Claude looks them up: their business, their content and what they've posted lately. You get a one-page brief with a fit verdict and the questions to ask.

Google Calendar Web search

What you get

  • A snapshot of their business and who they serve
  • What they've posted or announced recently
  • A fit score against your ideal client, with the reason
  • Three sharp questions to open the call

Set it up

  1. Connect Google Calendar in Settings then Connectors and make sure web search is switched on.
  2. Write two or three lines describing your ideal client and paste them into the prompt.
  3. Run the prompt once to test, then schedule it every weekday morning so each day's calls are researched before you start.
Prompt
Check my Google Calendar for sales calls booked in the next 48 hours. Sales calls are any events booked through my scheduling link or with "discovery", "strategy" or "intro" in the title. For each person, search the web for their name, company and website. Look at their site, LinkedIn, Instagram, YouTube and any recent news. My ideal client: who you serve, their size, the problem you solve Give me one page per person: - SNAPSHOT: What their business does, who it serves, rough size. - RECENT: What they've posted or announced in the last 60 days. - FIT: Strong, Maybe or Weak against my ideal client, with one line on why. - ASK: Three questions to open the call, based on what you found. If you can't find reliable information on someone, say so instead of guessing.

04

Your competitors, content and customers, researched in one go

Claude pulls public posts and comments from Instagram, TikTok, Reddit and review sites, then gives you a month of research back as one report.

Apify

What you get

  • What your competitors are posting and selling right now
  • Which posts in your niche went viral this month, and why
  • The exact words your buyers use when they complain

Set it up

  1. Create a free account at apify.com. Apify runs the scrapers that collect public posts.
  2. Connect Apify in Settings then Connectors in Claude and sign in.
  3. List your competitor handles, two or three niche hashtags and the subreddits your buyers use.
  4. Paste the prompt below with your list filled in.
Prompt
Use Apify to research my market for the last 30 days. Competitors: Instagram and TikTok handles Niche hashtags: 2 or 3 hashtags Subreddits: where your buyers hang out Review sources: Google, Trustpilot, Amazon or G2 pages Pick the right Apify actors for each source and keep each run small (about 50 posts per source) so it stays cheap. Report back in four sections: 1. COMPETITORS: What each one posted most, what they're selling or promoting, and their best performing post. 2. VIRAL: The 10 top posts in my niche by engagement, with the hook and one line on why it worked. 3. BUYER LANGUAGE: Complaints and frustrations from comments, Reddit and reviews. Quote them word for word and group by theme. 4. GAPS: Topics buyers keep asking about that nobody is covering well.

Warning

Apify charges per run from your account credits. Keep post limits small on your first run and check your usage in Apify before you schedule this.
05

Catch the dying ad before your costs jump

Every Monday, Claude flags the ads people are getting tired of and the one ad worth putting more money behind. Then it writes new angles based on what's already working.

Meta Ads

What you get every Monday

  • Ads showing fatigue: frequency going up while clicks go down
  • The single ad most worth scaling, with the numbers behind it
  • Fresh ad angles built from your winners

Set it up

  1. Connect your Meta Ads account. Check the Connectors directory first. If it isn't there, connect it through Composio or Zapier and add that as a custom connector.
  2. Give the connection read-only access where you can. This hack never needs to edit your account.
  3. Run the prompt once, then schedule it every Monday at 8am.
Prompt
Pull performance for every active ad in my Meta Ads account. Compare the last 7 days with the 7 days before. For each ad show: spend, frequency, CTR, CPC and cost per result. 1. FATIGUE: Flag any ad where frequency went up and CTR dropped by 20% or more. Say how many days it has left before it gets expensive. 2. SCALE: Name the one ad most worth more budget. Show the numbers that prove it. 3. NEW ANGLES: Look at the hooks, offers and formats of my 3 best ads. Write 5 new ad angles that keep what's working. For each give a hook line, the main message and a format idea. Report only. Do not change budgets, pause ads or edit anything in my account.
06

Find out which emails make you money

Claude reads every email you've sent this year, compares opens, clicks and sales, then drafts next week's emails using what worked.

Your email platform Or a CSV export

What you get

  • The subject lines, topics and send days that pay off
  • The ones your list ignores, so you can stop sending them
  • Next week's emails, drafted from your own winners

Set it up

  1. Check the Connectors directory for your email platform (Klaviyo, Mailchimp, Kit, GoHighLevel and others) and connect it if it's listed.
  2. If it isn't listed, export your campaign report for this year as a CSV. Include subject line, send date, opens, clicks and revenue.
  3. Paste the prompt, attaching the CSV if you exported one.
Prompt
Analyse every email campaign I've sent this year (from my email platform or the attached CSV). 1. MONEY: Rank campaigns by revenue, then by clicks. Show the top 10 and bottom 10. 2. PATTERNS: Which subject line styles, topics, lengths and send days get the most opens, clicks and sales? Back each pattern with numbers. 3. IGNORED: Which types of email get opened but never clicked, or barely opened at all? 4. NEXT WEEK: Draft 3 emails for next week using the winning patterns. Give each a subject line, preview text and full body, written in the same voice as my best performers. Our offer right now: what you're selling this month

07

The scope creep catcher

Claude reads new client emails against the scope you both signed. When a request falls outside it, Claude flags it and drafts a friendly reply with a price.

Gmail Your signed contract

What you get

  • Every out-of-scope request flagged with the clause it falls outside
  • A ready-to-send reply that says yes and names the price

Set it up

  1. Create a Claude Project for each client and upload the signed contract or scope of work to the project files.
  2. Add your rates to the project instructions, for example your hourly rate and prices for common add-ons.
  3. Connect Gmail in Settings then Connectors.
  4. Inside the project, run the prompt below. Schedule it daily if the client emails often.
Prompt
Search my Gmail for emails from client email domain in the last 7 days. Compare every request in those emails with the signed scope in this project's files. For each request, tell me: - IN SCOPE or OUT OF SCOPE, and which part of the contract decides it. - If out of scope: a rough time estimate and the price using my rates. For every out-of-scope request, draft a friendly reply that: - says I'm happy to help, - explains it sits outside our current agreement, - gives the price and timeline, - asks them to confirm before I start. Keep the tone warm. Draft only, do not send.
08

The monthly report written in ten minutes

Claude pulls the month's numbers from your client's sheets, analytics or ad account and writes the report in plain English, wins first.

Google Sheets Analytics Ad account

What you get

  • A client-ready report in plain English
  • Wins first, then what changed, then what you recommend next

Set it up

  1. Connect Google Drive so Claude can read the client's sheets. Connect their analytics or ad account too if a connector exists.
  2. For anything without a connector, export last month's numbers as a CSV and attach it.
  3. Save the prompt in the client's Claude Project so next month takes one click.
Prompt
Write the monthly report for client name covering month. Data sources: sheet name, analytics, ad account or attached CSV Structure: 1. WINS: The 3 best results this month, each with the number and the change from last month. 2. WHAT CHANGED: Anything that went up or down by more than 10%, and the likely reason. 3. WHAT'S NEXT: 2 or 3 recommendations for next month, each tied to a number in this report. Rules: plain English, no jargon, short paragraphs. Write it so a busy business owner gets the point in 60 seconds. Put a 3-line summary at the very top.
09

The retainer hours tracker

Each week Claude checks your calendar and email, tallies your time per client against what the retainer covers, and drafts the overage invoice when a client goes over.

Google Calendar Gmail Retainer terms

What you get every Friday

  • Hours spent per client this week and month to date
  • How far each client is over or under their retainer
  • A drafted overage invoice for anyone who went over

Set it up

  1. Put the client's name in the title of every calendar block you work for them. Claude matches time to clients by name.
  2. Write each client's retainer hours and overage rate in one place, such as a project file or the top of the prompt.
  3. Connect Google Calendar and Gmail, then schedule the prompt for Friday afternoons.
Prompt
Track my client hours for this week and this month so far. Retainers: client name: hours per month, overage rate per hour 1. CALENDAR: Add up the time on every event with a client's name in the title. 2. EMAIL: Count the threads I replied to for each client this week and estimate 5 minutes per reply. Show this as a separate line so I can adjust it. 3. TABLE: For each client show hours this week, hours this month, retainer hours and the difference. 4. OVERAGE: For any client over their retainer, say by how much and draft a short invoice note listing the extra hours, the rate and the total. Draft only, do not send anything.

Note

Email time is an estimate. Check the email line before you send an overage invoice, and adjust the 5 minute figure to match how you actually work.

Questions

Before you connect anything

How do I connect Claude to Gmail, Google Calendar, Slack or Asana?

Open Claude, go to Settings, then Connectors, find the app in the directory and sign in to authorise it. Once connected, Claude can read from that app inside any chat where the connector is turned on.

Will Claude send emails or change my accounts without asking?

Every prompt here tells Claude to draft and report only. You review the drafts and send them yourself. Claude also asks for approval before taking actions in connected apps unless you change that setting.

What if my tool has no Claude connector?

Export the data as a CSV or PDF and upload it to the chat or a Claude Project. The prompts work the same way on uploaded files. You can also bridge the tool through an integration platform such as Composio or Zapier.

How do I make a hack run every morning or every Monday?

Run the prompt once in a chat to check the output, then ask Claude to set it up as a scheduled task with the day and time you want. Each run starts fresh, so the prompt must contain everything Claude needs.

Do I need a paid Claude plan?

Connectors and scheduled tasks depend on your plan. Check the features listed for your plan in Claude settings before you set these up.
