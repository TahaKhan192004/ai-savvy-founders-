Welcome

How to use this guide

The seven tools fit together as one lead-gen pipeline: find businesses and profiles, crawl and enrich the data, verify emails, reach out, automate the repetitive steps, and support the leads who reply.

Pick the tools that match the stage you are working on. Always read each repo's README and license before you deploy, since features and licensing can change.

01 Stage: Find

Google Maps Scraper

GitHub repositorygithub.com/gosom/google-maps-scraper

What it does: pulls business listings from Google Maps, including names, addresses, phone numbers, websites and reviews, and exports them to CSV or JSON.

Use cases

  • Building local business lead lists (agencies, clinics, restaurants, salons, contractors).
  • Market research on competitors in a city or niche.
  • Collecting websites to feed into an enrichment or email-finding step.

When to use it

At the very start of a local B2B lead-gen flow, when you need a list of businesses in a specific niche and location.

Advantages

  • Free and self-hosted, with no per-lead pricing.
  • Structured output (CSV or JSON) that plugs straight into other tools.
  • Captures contact details and website URLs, which are the inputs for the next steps.
02 Stage: Find

Scout

GitHub repositorygithub.com/kiryano/Scout

What it does: scrapes Instagram, TikTok, Twitch and LinkedIn profiles, pulls emails from bios, verifies them via SMTP and exports to CSV.

Use cases

  • Finding creators and influencers for partnerships or sponsorships.
  • Building outreach lists from social profiles.
  • Collecting public contact emails from creator bios.

When to use it

When your target audience lives on social platforms (creators, coaches, founders, streamers) rather than in business directories.

Advantages

  • Covers several platforms in one tool.
  • Extracts and verifies emails in the same run.
  • CSV export makes it easy to move leads into a CRM or outreach tool.
03 Stage: Enrich

Crawl4AI

GitHub repositorygithub.com/unclecode/crawl4ai

What it does: turns any website into clean, LLM-ready Markdown for AI agents and RAG.

Use cases

  • Crawling a prospect's website to understand what they do before personalizing outreach.
  • Feeding website content to an LLM for summaries, lead scoring or classification.
  • Building knowledge bases and RAG pipelines from web content.

When to use it

When you have a list of websites (for example from the Google Maps Scraper) and need their content in a format an AI model can read.

Advantages

  • Output is clean Markdown, so you spend fewer tokens and get better LLM results.
  • Built for AI workflows, not just raw scraping.
  • Free and self-hostable.
04 Stage: Verify

check-if-email-exists

GitHub repositorygithub.com/reacherhq/check-if-email-exists

What it does: checks whether an email address exists without sending an email.

Use cases

  • Cleaning an email list before a campaign.
  • Reducing bounce rates and protecting your sender reputation.
  • Validating emails collected from scraping or sign-up forms.

When to use it

After you collect emails and before you send anything. Never skip this step on a scraped list.

Advantages

  • No email is sent, so the person you are checking is never contacted.
  • Free alternative to paid verification services.
  • Can be self-hosted and used through its backend or as a library.
05 Stage: Reach out

OpenOutreach

GitHub repositorygithub.com/eracle/OpenOutreach

What it does: an AI agent that finds the B2B leads who fit your product, explains why, and emails them from your mailbox.

Use cases

  • Automated B2B prospecting and first-touch outreach.
  • Getting a reason for each match, so you know why a lead fits.
  • Running outreach from your own mailbox instead of a third-party sender.

When to use it

After you know your ideal customer and have verified contacts, when you want to automate the first outreach step.

Advantages

  • Explains why each lead is a fit, which helps with personalization.
  • Sends from your own mailbox, which keeps replies in your normal inbox.
  • Free and open source, with no per-seat outreach fees.
06 Stage: Automate

Huginn

GitHub repositorygithub.com/huginn/huginn

What it does: lets you build agents that watch the web and act on your behalf, 24/7.

Use cases

  • Monitoring websites, job boards, news or social feeds for buying signals.
  • Sending alerts or digests when something changes.
  • Chaining scheduled tasks together, similar to a self-hosted IFTTT or Zapier.

When to use it

When you want a recurring, always-on process, such as tracking competitors or watching for new leads, without manual checking.

Advantages

  • Runs continuously on your own server.
  • Highly flexible, since agents can be chained into custom workflows.
  • You own the data and the infrastructure.
07 Stage: Support

Chatwoot

GitHub repositorygithub.com/chatwoot/chatwoot

What it does: an open-source live-chat and support desk, and an alternative to Intercom and Zendesk.

Use cases

  • Live chat on your website to capture and qualify inbound leads.
  • A shared inbox for your team to handle replies and support.
  • Customer support across multiple channels in one place.

When to use it

When leads start replying or visiting your site and you need one organized place to manage those conversations.

Advantages

  • Free, self-hostable alternative to paid support platforms.
  • Keeps all conversations in a single shared inbox.
  • Customizable, and you keep your customer data.

The SOP

Standard workflow

  1. Define your target. Pick one niche, one location or platform, and one offer.
  2. Collect leads. Run Google Maps Scraper for local businesses, or Scout for social profiles. Export to CSV.
  3. Enrich. Use Crawl4AI to read each prospect's website, then have an LLM summarize what they do and note a personalization angle.
  4. Verify. Run every email through check-if-email-exists. Remove invalid and risky addresses.
  5. Reach out. Use OpenOutreach to contact the leads that fit, from your own mailbox.
  6. Automate. Set up Huginn agents to watch for new leads, changes or signals, and to alert you.
  7. Manage replies. Route conversations and inbound chats through Chatwoot.
  8. Review weekly. Track replies, bounces and booked calls, then refine your target and messaging.

Stay safe

Best practices and compliance

  • Respect platform terms. Scraping Google Maps and social platforms can violate their terms of service. Keep request rates low and use the data responsibly.
  • Follow email laws. Comply with CAN-SPAM, GDPR and any rules in your target region. Include an unsubscribe option and honor opt-outs.
  • Warm up your mailbox. Start with small daily volumes and increase gradually to protect deliverability.
  • Collect only public data and store it securely.
  • Check each repo's license before using it commercially, since open-source licenses differ.
  • Test small first. Run each tool on 20 to 50 leads before scaling up.

Remember

Verify before you send, start small, and honor every opt-out.
