Before you start

The short answer

A strong AI poster prompt uses eight blocks in a fixed order: format, concept, hero subject, composition, typography, palette, material and light, then copy and negatives. Quality comes from the thought inside each block, not from prompt length.

  • Slop says everything at once.
  • Decent says one thing neatly.
  • Amazing says one thing in a way the viewer has not seen before.
Purpose

Give you a repeatable method for prompting posters that read as designed, with a side-by-side baseline so you can see the difference.

Who this is for

Founders and marketers making event, launch or content posters with AI image tools, no design background needed.

Tools needed

An image model that renders text well: ChatGPT images, Gemini, Higgsfield or similar. Plus one topic and ten minutes.

01

Write the exact copy

Every word on the poster gets decided before you touch the design. If you leave words out, the model invents them and misspells them.

  1. Pick a topic: an event, a product launch, a sport or a talk.
  2. Write the title exactly as it should appear.
  3. Write the date and venue.
  4. Write one tagline of three to six words.
Important
Keep this copy identical across all three tiers. When the words match, the only variable left is design quality, and that is the comparison you want.
02

Fill the 8 blocks

Write one to two sentences per block, in this order. Models weigh early words most, so format and concept go first and negatives go last.

BlockWhat to writeExample
1FormatRatio, medium and render mode in one line"Vertical 3:4 poster, photoreal studio photograph"
2ConceptThe single idea, described as a picture"A match burns down like a countdown timer"
3Hero subjectThe one thing the eye lands on: object, pose, scale, camera angle"A single white pawn, low angle, lit from the left"
4CompositionWhere things sit, what overlaps, what is cropped, how much space is empty"Giant headline tucked behind the figure, top 60% empty black"
5TypographyHeadline words, weight, size, and how the type interacts with the image"Heavy condensed white capitals, cropped at the top edge"
6PaletteTwo to four named colors and their ratio"Black, white, one vermilion accent"
7Material and lightSurface texture, print finish, lighting, lens"Risograph grain, hard side light, shallow depth of field"
8Copy and negativesEvery word in quotes, then what to leave out"Text: 'Not yet.' No icons, no stickers, no gradients"

Rules for every block

  1. Be specific. "Red leather boxing glove with visible stitching" beats "a boxing glove".
  2. Name the exact copy. Put every word you want rendered in quotes.
  3. Keep one hero. Two things competing for attention reads as Slop.
03

Know the three tiers

The tiers differ in concept and restraint, not prompt length. Use this table as your reference while writing Steps 4 to 8.

04

Write the Slop version

Write this one first and use the clichés on purpose. It sets your baseline and surfaces the exact clichés you will ban later.

  1. Copy the template below.
  2. Swap every [bracket] with your topic's details and clichés.
  3. Write down the three clichés you used. They become your negative line in Steps 5 and 8.
Slop template
Vertical 3:4 [topic] poster, glossy [two neon colors] gradient style.
Giant chrome title "[TITLE]" with "[CLICHE TAGLINE]" below it.
[Most obvious cliché image] in the center, [second cliché] on the right, floating icons and sparkles around it.
Five boxed info cards with tiny icons: "[FEATURE 1]", "[FEATURE 2]", "[FEATURE 3]", "[FEATURE 4]", "[FEATURE 5]".
Info strip: "[DATE]", "[VENUE]", "[PRICE]".
Burst stickers "[URGENT PHRASE 1]!" and "[URGENT PHRASE 2]!".
Footer line "[CLICHE SLOGAN]" and a URL bar.
Crowded, shiny, lots of small text.
05

Write the Decent version

Decent is clean, correct and calm. It picks one style and applies it with discipline.

  1. Choose one style: flat vector, Swiss grid, mid-century or isometric.
  2. Pick one subject and one position for it.
  3. Name three colors plus one accent.
  4. Fill the template below with the same copy from Step 1.
Decent template
Vertical 3:4 [topic] poster in a clean [style: flat vector / Swiss grid / mid-century / isometric] look on a [background color] background.
One clear [subject] in the [position].
Bold [font style] title "[TITLE]" at the top, "[SHORT TAGLINE]" beneath it in a lighter weight, then "[DATE] / [VENUE] / [URL]" in small spaced capitals at the bottom.
Strict grid, generous margins.
Palette: [color 1], [color 2], [color 3], one [accent] accent.
Light paper grain. Polished and calm.
Warning
A Decent prompt without a named grid, type hierarchy and exact palette lets the model pick a stock arrangement. The result looks like a template.
06

Invent the Amazing concept

An Amazing poster has one idea that explains the topic at a glance. Start here, because no amount of texture rescues a weak idea.

  1. List the clichés. Write the three most obvious images for your topic. These become your negative line.
  2. Pick the object. Choose the one object that most directly stands for the topic.
  3. Apply one twist from the table below.
  4. Write the tagline. Three to six words that complete the idea.
  5. Run the relevance test. Remove the title in your head. Would someone still guess the topic? If the twist needs a prop unrelated to the topic, drop it.

Six twists that work

TwistHow it worksExample
SubstitutionReplace one expected thing with anotherA pawn whose shadow is a queen
ScaleMake something tiny or enormousA giant hand placing a stone over a city
MaterialBuild the subject from an unexpected materialArms made of braided cables arm-wrestling
TimeFreeze a moment or show a countdownA burning match as a three-minute timer
ContrastBreak one element out of a patternOne red runner against a river of runners
FramePut a window or frame inside the imageA film frame cut from black card showing a different sky
Tip
A tagline works when it completes the idea. Try a rhythm of three ("Point. Click. Win."), a quiet line ("Not yet."), an invitation ("Leave a trace.") or a surprising fact ("More moves than atoms in the universe.").
07

Apply the craft levers

The concept makes a poster interesting. The levers make it feel crafted. Use at least three on every Amazing prompt.

LeverWhat it meansPrompt wording
LayeringType and subject overlap in depth"headline tucked behind the subject, letters crossing the figure"
Tactile materialThe image feels printed or photographed, not rendered"halftone dots, risograph grain, paper texture, film grain"
Interacting propOne element links the subject to the type or frame"a cable loops around the subject and through the letters"
Scale or perspectiveA viewpoint the viewer does not see every day"top-down aerial, low angle, macro, tiny figure beside a monolith"
Note
Palette formula for Amazing: one base color, one ink color, one accent. Never more.
08

Assemble the Amazing prompt

  1. Copy the template below.
  2. Drop in your concept and twist from Step 6.
  3. Add your three or four levers from Step 7.
  4. Close with the negative line built from your Slop clichés.
Amazing template
Vertical 3:4 [format: photoreal studio photograph / graphic halftone cutout / cinematic film still] on [flat or scene background].
[HERO SUBJECT with specific material, pose and camera angle].
[THE TWIST: one sentence from the six twists].
[INTERACTING PROP linking subject to type].
Giant [weight] [color] type "[HEADLINE]" [layering: tucked behind / cropped at the top edge / part of the scene].
Small [serif/sans] text [position]: "[TAGLINE]".
Tiny spaced capitals [position]: "[DATE] / [VENUE]".
[MATERIAL: halftone / riso grain / film grain / hard single light].
Only [color A], [color B], [color C].
[Negative line].
09

Generate all three

  1. Paste your three prompts into the combined template below.
  2. Run it in your image model.
  3. If your tool returns only one image per request, run it again with "Generate only Image 2", then again with "Generate only Image 3".
  4. Put the three posters side by side and compare.
Three-poster prompt
Create THREE separate vertical 3:4 posters (not a collage, not side by side) for the same [event/product]: "[NAME]", [DATE], [VENUE]. Same details and copy on all three, but a different design quality. Label them Image 1 (SLOP), Image 2 (DECENT), Image 3 (AMAZING).

IMAGE 1, SLOP: [paste Slop prompt]

IMAGE 2, DECENT: [paste Decent prompt]

IMAGE 3, AMAZING: [paste Amazing prompt]

Negative for all: [clichés to avoid on Images 2 and 3].

Worked example

City Marathon, start to finish

Topic: "CITY MARATHON 2026", [DATE], [VENUE], 42.195 KM.

  1. Clichés: a runner silhouette at sunrise, medal icons, a stopwatch. These go in the negative line.
  2. Object: the crowd of runners, because a marathon is mostly about the crowd.
  3. Twist: Contrast. One runner breaks out of a river of runners and goes the other way.
  4. Tagline: "Everyone runs the same distance. Nobody runs the same race."
  5. Relevance test: remove the title and a crowd of runners in a city still says marathon. It passes.

2ConceptA river of runners flows down a city avenue and one runner goes against it
3Hero subjectOne runner in signal red running upstream, leaving a thin red trail
4CompositionGiant "42.195" cropped off the top edge and tucked behind the buildings
5TypographyHeavy black grotesque, small "KM" beside the number
6PaletteGray, black, cream, one signal red
7Material and lightDawn light, fine film grain, paper texture
8Copy and negativesTagline and details in quotes. No sunrise silhouette, no medal icons, no stopwatch

Troubleshooting

When the poster falls flat

Weak posters usually trace back to one of five fixable habits.

SymptomWhy it failsFix
A gimmick unrelated to the topicThe twist is funny but explains nothing, so the poster feels randomRun the relevance test: remove the title and see if the topic is still obvious
"Make it look cool"The model fills the gaps with its default clichésName the material, light, palette and layout instead of adjectives
One object, three wordsReads as Decent: clean but flatAdd layering, a tactile material and an interacting prop
Decent that looks like a templateNo layout rule, so the model picks a stock arrangementName the grid, the type hierarchy and the exact palette
Garbled small textToo many words, too smallQuote only the title and one tagline in full, call the fine print "tiny placeholder text"
FAQs

Common questions

What is the 8-block poster prompt structure?
Format, concept, hero subject, composition, typography, palette, material and light, then copy and negatives. Write them in that order because image models weigh early words most.
Does a longer prompt make a better AI poster?
No. The three tiers differ in concept and restraint, not length. The thought inside each block decides quality.
How do I stop the model from misspelling poster text?
Quote every word you want rendered, keep full quotes to the title and one tagline, and describe the fine print as tiny placeholder text.
How many colors should an Amazing poster use?
Two to three: one base color, one ink color and one accent. Decent posters use three to four.
Why write a Slop version on purpose?
It gives you a baseline and surfaces the topic's clichés, which become the negative line for the Decent and Amazing versions.
Which image models work best?
Any model that renders text well, such as ChatGPT images, Gemini or Higgsfield.
