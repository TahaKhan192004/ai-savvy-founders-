The Repurposing Machine: Automate Your Content Distribution

Purpose

This SOP walks you through building a daily content repurposing routine that takes what you already wrote, rewrites it for each platform in your voice, and publishes it on a schedule with you nowhere near it.

Who this is for: Founders who post to at least two platforms and want to stop rewriting the same content by hand every day.

Tools needed:

  • An AI assistant that can run on a schedule and connect to other tools
  • A content scheduler with your social accounts connected
  • Somewhere your finished copy already lands (project board, notes app, or document)
01

Understand the Four Parts

Whatever tools you pick, every repurposing system is built from the same four components. Once you can name them, this stops feeling technical.

Source

The place your finished writing already lands. A project board, a notes app, a doc. Somewhere you already use, not somewhere new.

Voice

Your rules for how you write. The words you refuse to use, how you open, paragraph length, what your call to action looks like.

Publisher

A scheduler holding the accounts you want to post to. It takes the finished posts and puts them out at the time you picked.

Memory

A list of what has already gone out. Without it, the routine finds your newest piece every single day and posts it again.

Note

The "memory" is what this SOP calls a ledger in the template below. It is just a simple file that records the ID of each content item after it has been published.

02

Choose Your Content Source

Look at where your finished captions and posts actually sit right now, and use that. A fresh folder created specifically for this automation is empty inside two weeks, every time.

Your source needs to be somewhere that fills itself as a side effect of work you are already doing. If you write captions in Notion, use Notion. If your content lives on a Trello board, use that board. If it sits in a Google Doc, point the routine there.

Warning

Do not create a new location "for the automation." Point the routine at where your content already lives. New, empty sources are the number one reason these setups go silent.

03

Map Where the Copy Lives

Open a few items in your source and note exactly where the finished writing sits. This is the step that decides whether any of it works, and it takes about four minutes.

  1. Open three to five recent content items in your source.
  2. Find the exact field that holds the finished caption or post copy. Is it a description field? A comment? A specific column? A note body?
  3. Check whether the field name is consistent across items. In many project boards, one task list keeps copy in the comments and another keeps it in the description.
  4. Write down the field name exactly as the tool labels it. You will use this in Step 6.
Important

Point at the wrong field and the routine tells you there is nothing to post while you are looking at a full board. This can run for two weeks before anybody questions it.

04

Connect Your Scheduler

Do this before you write any instructions, because whatever the scheduler cannot post to is not going to happen.

  1. Open your scheduler (e.g. Late, Buffer, Publer, or whichever tool you use).
  2. Connect every social account you want the routine to post to.
  3. Verify each connection by posting a test or checking the account status shows "connected."
  4. Note the exact platform names as they appear in the scheduler. You will use these in the template.
Tip

Check that your platforms are properly supported by the scheduler and not just mentioned somewhere on a pricing page. Some schedulers list platforms they integrate with only on higher-tier plans.

05

Write Your Voice Rules

This file is the entire difference between your voice and generic AI output, and it is worth having even if you never automate anything.

Your voice rules should cover:

  1. Words you never use. List them explicitly. For example: "never use leverage, unlock, game-changing, or revolutionary."
  2. How you open. Do you start with a question? A statement? A single short sentence? Document your pattern.
  3. Paragraph length. How many lines before you break? One sentence per paragraph? Three max?
  4. Call to action style. What does your CTA look like? "Comment X below"? "Link in bio"? A question that prompts replies?
  5. Tone markers. Conversational or editorial? First person or second person? Short and punchy or measured and calm?
Tip

If you already have a voice skill or voice guide built, reference it by name in the template. If not, write 5 to 10 core rules and paste them directly into the prompt as a fallback.

06

Build the Prompt Template

Copy the template below. Replace everything in curly braces with your own answers. Paste it wherever your AI assistant runs on a schedule.

Prompt 
You are running my daily content repurposing routine.
Do it end to end. Never publish the same piece twice.

RULE ZERO
This runs unattended. Nobody is there to answer you.
Never ask a question. Never wait for approval.
Never stop because something is missing. Take the fallback and keep going.

1. LOAD THE LEDGER
Read {LEDGER FILE}. It lists the IDs already published.
If it does not exist, treat it as empty.

2. OPEN THE SOURCE
Go to {WHERE YOUR COPY LIVES}. Each item is one piece of content.

3. PICK ONE
Consider only items whose ID is not already in the ledger.
Work newest first.
The finished copy lives in {THE EXACT FIELD}.
Ignore {THE FIELD THAT HOLDS CHATTER AND LINKS}.
Never treat it as the copy.
Usable copy means a finished piece: an opening line,
real body text, a call to action.
Not a one line reminder, not a bare link, not a note to self.
If an item has no usable copy, skip it WITHOUT adding it
to the ledger, and try the next.
Process exactly one item per run. If nothing qualifies, stop and say so.

4. REWRITE IT
Voice: {YOUR VOICE RULES, OR THE NAME OF YOUR VOICE SKILL}
If those will not load, use this shortened version instead:
{5 TO 10 OF YOUR CORE RULES}
Never invent numbers, client results, testimonials, or dates.
Use only specifics that appear in the source copy.
Never leave a bracketed placeholder or a note to me inside the text.
If a detail is missing, cut the claim instead.
Write one version per platform:
   {PLATFORM 1}: {FORMAT AND LENGTH LIMIT}
   {PLATFORM 2}: {FORMAT AND LENGTH LIMIT}
Return only the finished posts.
No alternative openings, no explanation of your choices,
no labels or headings.

5. SCHEDULE
Send each post to {YOUR SCHEDULER} for {TIME} today,
set to publish automatically.
If that time has already passed, use tomorrow.
Confirm both came back scheduled before you continue.

6. RECORD IT
Only once both are confirmed, add this item's ID to the ledger.
Append to the list. Never overwrite it.

END OF RUN
Report which item you used and the scheduled post IDs.

Each placeholder maps to something you already noted in previous steps:

Placeholder What to put here Example
{LEDGER FILE} Path or name of the file tracking published items repurposing-ledger.md in my Claude project
{WHERE YOUR COPY LIVES} Your content source from Step 2 My "Content Ready" board in Notion
{THE EXACT FIELD} The field name from Step 3 The "Description" field of each card
{THE FIELD THAT HOLDS CHATTER AND LINKS} Any field that looks like copy but is not the final version The "Comments" section
{YOUR VOICE RULES} Your voice skill name or inline rules from Step 5 ai-savvy-voice skill
{PLATFORM 1} First target platform with format and length LinkedIn: text post, under 1300 characters
{YOUR SCHEDULER} The scheduler tool from Step 4 Late (via Zernio MCP connector)
{TIME} Your preferred daily publish time 12:00 PM GST
07

Schedule and Test

Set the routine to fire once a day at your preferred time, then run it once yourself before walking away.

  1. Set the schedule (e.g. every weekday at 9:00 AM) in whatever tool runs your AI assistant.
  2. Run the routine manually once, right now. This first manual run is where you approve the permissions it needs for your source, scheduler, and ledger file.
  3. Watch the output. Confirm it picked the right content item, rewrote it in your voice, and scheduled it to the correct accounts.
  4. Check the ledger file to confirm the item ID was recorded.
Warning

Skip the manual run and the first automatic run sits waiting for a permission click nobody is there to give. Always approve permissions first.

Troubleshooting

None of these failures throw an error. They quietly hand you a worse result than you think you are getting.

Symptom Cause Fix
The routine says "nothing to publish" but your board is full It is reading the wrong field. The copy is in Description but it is looking at Comments (or vice versa). Open a source item, confirm the exact field name, and update {THE EXACT FIELD} in your prompt.
Posts go out with working notes, alt openings, or framework labels attached The voice guide or writing prompt returns its reasoning alongside the finished post. Add explicit instructions: "Return only the finished posts. No alternative openings, no explanation of your choices, no labels or headings."
Posts contain fabricated numbers, revenue figures, or client results The AI filled gaps with invented specifics to make the copy feel more concrete. Add: "Never invent numbers, client results, testimonials, or dates. If a detail is missing, cut the claim instead."
The same content item gets posted every day The ledger is not being updated, or the routine overwrites it instead of appending. Confirm the ledger file exists and the prompt says "Append to the list. Never overwrite it."
The routine asks a question and stops Your voice guide opens by asking what you are writing and who it is for. That is right in a chat window and useless unattended. Put the answers directly in the prompt and include Rule Zero: "Never ask a question. Never wait for approval."
