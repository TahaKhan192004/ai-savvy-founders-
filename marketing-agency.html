Overview

What this SOP covers

Agency owners lose hours every month to client reporting, chasing proposals and figuring out which retainers still make money. This SOP maps 12 AI systems onto the tools your team already uses, so the delivery work runs without someone sitting in the middle.

Purpose

  • Cut manual hours across sales, onboarding and delivery
  • Catch margin leakage and at-risk clients early
  • Give every team member instant answers from approved sources

Who this is for

  • Agency owners and founders
  • Operations and delivery leads
  • Account managers and sales leads
  • The person building the automations

Tools needed

CRM Email platform Project tool Analytics and ad platforms Time tracker Slack Automation layer (n8n, Make or Zapier) LLM (Claude or similar)

Important

Every client-facing output in this SOP is saved as a draft for a human to approve. Scores, alerts and internal summaries can run on their own. Client emails, reports and change orders never send without an account lead clicking approve.

01Sales

Lead qualification

Checks every new lead against your ideal client profile, scores it and routes it to the right salesperson.

Trigger
New contact or form submission in the CRM
Tools
CRM, LLM, Slack
Output
Score, reason and owner on the contact record
  1. Write your ICP rubric. List the factors that make a client a fit and give each a point value:
    • Industry and niche
    • Monthly budget range
    • Service they need vs services you sell
    • Company size and team
    • Timeline and urgency
  2. Create the trigger. In your automation layer, start the workflow when a new contact is created or a lead form is submitted.
  3. Enrich the lead. Pull the company website, LinkedIn page and any form answers into one block of text.
  4. Score with the LLM. Send the lead data and rubric to the LLM using the prompt below. Write the score and reason back to custom fields on the contact.
  5. Route by score.
    • 70 and above: assign to the salesperson for that service line and post to the sales Slack channel
    • 40 to 69: add to a nurture sequence
    • Below 40: tag as "not a fit" and send a polite referral email
Prompt
You are the lead qualifier for a marketing agency. Score this lead from 0 to 100 using the rubric below. Return JSON only: {"score": number, "service_line": string, "reason": string} RUBRIC: - Industry fit (0-25): [list your target industries] - Budget fit (0-25): monthly budget at or above [your minimum] - Service fit (0-20): needs [your core services] - Size fit (0-15): [your ideal team size or revenue range] - Urgency (0-15): wants to start within 60 days LEAD DATA: {{lead_data}} Keep "reason" to two sentences a salesperson can read in five seconds.
02Onboarding

Proposal to project

Sets up the welcome email, questionnaire, access requests and project tasks the moment a proposal is signed.

Trigger
Proposal signed or deal moved to Won
Tools
CRM, email, project tool, Slack
Output
Client onboarded, project live with tasks and owners
  1. Build one project template per service. Include every onboarding task, owner role and due date offset (for example, kickoff call on day 3).
  2. Set the trigger. Fire the workflow when the deal stage changes to won or the e-signature tool returns "signed".
  3. Send the welcome email. Personalise it with the client name, account lead and kickoff booking link.
  4. Send the onboarding questionnaire. Use a form that writes answers back to the CRM contact.
  5. Send access requests. One checklist covering ad accounts, analytics, website CMS, social profiles and brand assets.
  6. Create the project. Duplicate the matching template in your project tool, set the start date and assign the delivery team.
  7. Notify the team. Post the new client, services sold and kickoff date to the delivery Slack channel.

Tip

Add a reminder task for the account lead if access requests are still incomplete after 3 days. Missing access is the most common reason kickoffs slip.

03Sales

Proposal follow-up

Drafts a follow-up for every quiet proposal based on where the deal sits.

Trigger
Proposal sent with no reply after 3, 7 and 14 days
Tools
CRM, email, LLM
Output
Draft email and follow-up task for the deal owner
  1. Run a daily check. Search the CRM for deals at the proposal stage with no inbound reply in 3, 7 or 14 days.
  2. Gather context. Pull the deal stage, proposal value, services, last call notes and the last email thread.
  3. Draft by stage.
    • Day 3: a short check-in that answers a likely question
    • Day 7: a relevant result or case study from a similar client
    • Day 14: a clear yes, no or later question
  4. Save as a draft. Put the email in the deal owner's drafts and create a CRM task to review it.
  5. Stop on reply. End the sequence as soon as the prospect replies or the deal changes stage.
04Delivery

Client reporting and diagnosis

Pulls campaign data into a report, flags anomalies and drafts next actions.

Trigger
Scheduled: weekly or monthly per client
Tools
Ad platforms, analytics, LLM, email
Output
Draft report with flagged issues and recommended actions
  1. Set targets per client. Store each client's KPIs (cost per lead, ROAS, conversion rate) in the CRM or a config sheet.
  2. Pull the data. On schedule, fetch spend, clicks, conversions and revenue for the period and the previous period.
  3. Flag anomalies. Mark any metric that moved more than 20% vs last period or sits outside its target.
  4. Draft the diagnosis. Ask the LLM to explain each flagged change in plain language and suggest specific next actions.
  5. Send for review. Save the report as a draft and notify the account manager in Slack. They edit, approve and send.

Warning

Give the LLM the raw numbers and tell it never to invent figures. Have it quote the exact metric next to every claim so the account manager can verify in seconds.

05Finance

Profitability monitor

Compares hours, deliverables and retainer value to flag margin leakage after each month closes.

Trigger
Scheduled: first working day of each month
Tools
Time tracker, project tool, CRM, Slack
Output
Margin table per client with leakage flags
  1. Pull hours. Export last month's tracked hours per client from the time tracker.
  2. Pull deliverables. Count completed tasks or deliverables per client from the project tool.
  3. Pull retainer value. Get each client's monthly fee from the CRM.
  4. Calculate margin. Effective hourly rate = retainer value / hours. Margin = retainer minus (hours x blended team cost).
  5. Flag and report. Mark any client below your target rate and post the summary to the leadership Slack channel.
06Team

Agency knowledge assistant

Answers team questions from approved SOPs, brand rules and agency policies.

Trigger
Team member asks a question in Slack
Tools
Slack, document store, LLM
Output
Answer in thread with the source document linked
  1. Collect approved documents. Put SOPs, brand rules, templates, pricing and process docs in one folder.
  2. Index the folder. Connect it to a retrieval setup so the assistant searches documents before answering.
  3. Add the Slack bot. Let team members mention the bot or use a dedicated #ask-ops channel.
  4. Restrict answers to sources. Instruct the assistant to say "I couldn't find that in our docs" when nothing matches.
  5. Review monthly. Log unanswered questions and write the missing SOPs.
07Sales

Pitch research agent

Reads a prospect's site, ads, reviews and socials, writes a one-page audit with pitch angles and attaches it to the CRM deal.

Trigger
Deal created or moved to Discovery Booked
Tools
CRM, web scraper, ad library, LLM
Output
One-page audit attached to the deal
  1. Capture the website URL. Make the domain a required field on the deal.
  2. Scrape public data. Collect the homepage and offer pages, active ads from public ad libraries, recent reviews and the latest social posts.
  3. Write the audit. Ask the LLM for a one-page summary: current positioning, gaps, quick wins and pitch angles for your services.
  4. Attach and notify. Save the audit as a note or file on the deal and send the salesperson a Slack message before the call.
08Delivery

Client knowledge assistant

Answers team questions in Slack from each client's briefs, call transcripts, brand guides and past reports, with the source document linked.

Trigger
Question asked in a client Slack channel
Tools
Slack, document store, call recorder, LLM
Output
Client-specific answer with source link
  1. Create one folder per client. Store briefs, brand guides, past reports and call transcripts there.
  2. Auto-file transcripts. Send every recorded client call transcript to that client's folder.
  3. Map channels to folders. A question in #client-acme only searches the Acme folder.
  4. Always cite. Every answer links the document it came from so the team can check it.

Note

System 06 covers how your agency works. System 08 covers what each client needs. Keep them as separate indexes so client details never leak into another client's answers.

09Delivery

Cross-channel client dashboard

Merges ad platforms, analytics and CRM revenue, writes the weekly commentary and sends a Slack alert when a metric moves.

Trigger
Daily data sync, weekly commentary
Tools
Ad platforms, analytics, CRM, database, Slack
Output
Live dashboard, weekly summary, threshold alerts
  1. Sync data daily. Pull spend and results from each ad platform, sessions and conversions from analytics and closed revenue from the CRM into one table.
  2. Build the dashboard. One view per client showing spend, leads, cost per lead, revenue and ROAS by channel.
  3. Set alert thresholds. For example, cost per lead up 25% day over day or spend pacing 15% over budget.
  4. Send alerts. Post to the client Slack channel and tag the account lead when a threshold is crossed.
  5. Write weekly commentary. Every Monday, the LLM summarises the week per client and adds it to the dashboard.
10Creative

Creative performance analyzer

Tags every ad by hook, format, text and imagery, matches it to click-through and cost per lead, and turns the winning patterns into briefs.

Trigger
Scheduled: weekly
Tools
Ad platforms, LLM with vision, project tool
Output
Tagged ad library and new creative briefs
  1. Pull every live ad. Get the creative file, copy and performance data for each ad.
  2. Tag each ad. Ask the LLM to label the hook type, format (static, video, carousel), on-screen text and main imagery.
  3. Match tags to results. Group by tag and compare average click-through rate and cost per lead.
  4. Find winning patterns. Surface tag combinations that beat the account average with enough spend behind them.
  5. Create briefs. Write a creative brief for each winning pattern and add it as a task in the project tool for the creative team.
11Retention

Retainer churn predictor

Scores each client on reply times, email sentiment, report opens and missed deliverables, and flags at-risk accounts with a suggested save action.

Trigger
Scheduled: weekly
Tools
CRM, email, project tool, LLM
Output
Health score and at-risk flag on the CRM record
  1. Collect the signals.
    • Average client reply time over the last 30 days
    • Sentiment of recent client emails (LLM-scored)
    • Whether the last reports were opened
    • Missed or late deliverables from the project tool
  2. Weight and score. Combine the signals into a 0 to 100 health score using the weights below as a starting point.
  3. Flag at-risk accounts. Below 50 sets an at-risk tag in the CRM.
  4. Suggest a save action. The LLM reads the signals and proposes one specific action, such as a strategy call or a quick-win deliverable.
  5. Assign it. Create a task for the account lead with the save action and due date.
Config
{ "health_score_weights": { "reply_time": 0.25, "email_sentiment": 0.30, "report_opens": 0.20, "missed_deliverables": 0.25 }, "at_risk_threshold": 50, "watch_threshold": 65 }
12Finance

Scope and margin guard

Compares time-tracker hours with retainer value mid-month, flags scope creep and drafts a change order for the account lead.

Trigger
Scheduled: 15th of each month
Tools
Time tracker, CRM, project tool, LLM
Output
Scope alert and draft change order
  1. Set hour budgets. For every retainer, record the monthly hour allowance in the CRM.
  2. Check mid-month. On the 15th, compare hours logged so far against the allowance.
  3. Flag scope creep. Over 60% of the allowance used by mid-month, or tasks logged that sit outside the signed scope.
  4. Draft the change order. The LLM writes a change order listing the extra work, hours and price.
  5. Send to the account lead. They review, adjust and decide whether to send it to the client.

Note

System 05 looks back at a closed month. System 12 runs while there is still time to act on the current month. Use both.

FAQs

Questions agency owners ask

Do I need to build all 12 systems at once?
No. Start with the system closest to your biggest time drain. For delivery teams that is usually client reporting (System 04) or proposal to project (System 02). Each system works on its own.
Which tools does my agency need?
A CRM, an email platform, a project tool, your ad and analytics platforms, a time tracker and Slack. An automation layer such as n8n, Make or Zapier connects them, and an LLM handles scoring, drafting and summarising.
Will the AI send emails or reports to clients without checking?
Not in this SOP. Follow-ups, reports and change orders are saved as drafts for the account lead to approve. Internal scores and alerts run on their own.
How long does setup take?
Trigger-based systems such as proposal to project take a few days. Data-heavy systems such as the cross-channel dashboard or churn predictor take one to two weeks each. All 12 take roughly six weeks.
What is the difference between the profitability monitor and the scope guard?
The profitability monitor reviews the full month after it closes and shows which clients lose money. The scope and margin guard runs mid-month and drafts a change order before the overrun happens.

Troubleshooting

When something breaks

ProblemLikely causeFix
Lead scores feel randomRubric is vague or missing point valuesAdd clear criteria and points per factor. Test on 20 past leads you already know the outcome of.
Reports show wrong numbersDate ranges or time zones differ between platformsSet one reporting time zone and pull every platform with the same start and end date.
Knowledge assistant makes things upNo instruction to stay within sourcesRequire a source link for every answer and a "not found" reply when nothing matches.
Margin figures look offTeam logs time without a client or projectMake client and project required fields in the time tracker.
Too many Slack alertsThresholds set too tightWiden thresholds and require a minimum spend or volume before an alert fires.
Project tasks not created after signingDeal stage name changed in the CRMUpdate the trigger to the new stage name and re-test with a dummy deal.
