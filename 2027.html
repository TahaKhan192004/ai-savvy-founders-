Start here

Let Claude interview you, then write your 2027

Paste one prompt into Claude. It asks you about ten to fifteen questions, one at a time, and then writes your year on a single page: goals with numbers and dates, monthly steps, the numbers to check, what is most likely to stop you, and what to do this Monday.

It does not hand you a generic template. The plan is built from your own answers, so it fits your real life.

What you get

  • An interview of around ten to fifteen questions, one at a time, that does not accept vague answers.
  • Your goals for the year, each with a number and a date.
  • Baby steps for every month, January to December.
  • The numbers to check each month so you know you are on track.
  • What is most likely to stop you, and what to do when it happens.
  • The first thing to do this Monday.
  • A plan short enough to read again in March.
01

How to use it

Four steps, no setup

  1. Set aside a quiet block of time. You will answer around ten to fifteen questions, so do not rush it.
  2. Open a new chat in Claude.
  3. Copy the prompt below and paste it in. You do not need to change anything.
  4. Answer each question honestly, in real numbers. When Claude finishes, read the one-page plan and save it.
Before you start

Think about what you want next year. It might be a health goal, an income goal, or something you have been putting off for years. You can give more than one, and Claude will help you choose the ones that matter most.

02

The prompt

Copy and paste

Prompt
I want you to interview me about my 2027 and then write my plan for the year.

Start by asking what I want next year. It might be a health goal, an income goal, or something I've been putting off for years. I can give you more than one.

Then interview me, one question at a time, and wait for my answer before asking the next one. Ask around ten to fifteen questions in total.

While you interview me:
- Don't accept a vague answer. If I say "get fit" or "earn more", ask me for a number and a date.
- Ask where I am right now with each goal, in real numbers.
- Ask what happened the last time I tried, and why I didn't do it this year.
- Ask what ideas I already have for making each goal happen.
- Ask what my normal week looks like, so the plan fits my real life.
- If I give you too many goals, make me choose the ones that matter most.
- Be direct with me. I'd rather hear an uncomfortable question than a polite one.

When you have enough, stop asking and write my 2027 on one page:
1. My goals for the year, each with a number and a date.
2. The baby steps I need to take each month, January to December.
3. The numbers I should check every month to know I'm on track.
4. What is most likely to stop me, and what to do when it happens.
5. The first thing to do this Monday.

Keep the plan short enough that I'd actually read it again in March.
03

What the interview does

How Claude will question you

The prompt tells Claude to ask one question at a time and wait for your answer. It also tells Claude to push back. Here is what to expect.

Claude willWhat that means for you
Not accept vague answersIf you say "get fit" or "earn more", it asks for a number and a date
Ask where you are right nowAnswer in real numbers: current weight, current income, current savings
Ask what happened last timeBe honest about why it did not work, and why you did not do it this year
Ask for your own ideasWhat have you already thought of for making each goal happen?
Ask about your normal weekSo the plan fits the time and energy you really have
Make you chooseIf you give too many goals, it makes you pick the ones that matter most
Be directIt would rather ask an uncomfortable question than a polite one

From vague to specific

If you sayClaude will ask for
Get fitA number and a date, such as a weight, a distance or a number of workouts a week, and by when
Earn moreHow much, per month or per year, and by when
Finally start the thing I keep putting offWhat exactly the finished thing is, and the date it should exist
Answer honestly

The plan is only as good as your answers. Give real numbers and real reasons, even when they are uncomfortable. Nobody else reads this.

04

Your plan

What the one-page plan contains

PartWhat it is
1. GoalsYour goals for the year, each with a number and a date
2. Monthly baby stepsThe small steps to take each month, January to December
3. Monthly numbersWhat to check every month to know you are on track
4. ObstaclesWhat is most likely to stop you, and what to do when it happens
5. This MondayThe first thing to do this Monday

When Claude writes the plan, read it carefully. If something does not fit your life, say so in the same chat and ask Claude to change it. The prompt asks for a short plan, so ask it to cut anything you would not actually read again.

After you have your plan

  • Save it somewhere you will see it: a document, your notes app, or printed out.
  • Do the Monday step before you do anything else.
  • Put the monthly check-in in your calendar. Pick one day each month to check your numbers.
  • Optional: add it to a Claude Project, so Claude already has your plan when you come back to it.
05

Optional

A monthly check-in prompt

Each month, paste your plan and your numbers back into Claude and ask for an honest read. This prompt is an extra, not part of the original, so edit it however you like.

Monthly check-in
Here is my 2027 plan: [paste your plan]. It's now [month]. Here are my numbers: [your numbers for the month]. Tell me honestly whether I'm on track, what to change, and what my one priority is for the next 30 days. Be direct.

Troubleshooting

If something goes wrong

ProblemFix
Claude asks several questions at onceReply: "One question at a time, please. Wait for my answer."
It accepts a vague answerSay: "Push me for a number and a date." It should ask again
I don't know my current numbersSay so. Ask Claude to help you work out how to measure them, then come back with the numbers
The interview feels too longYou can say "That's enough, write my plan now." The prompt asks for around ten to fifteen questions
The plan is too long or too genericAsk: "Cut it down to what I'd actually read in March, and use my own answers and numbers."
I want to change a goalTell Claude in the same chat and ask it to rewrite the plan with the new goal

You're set

One prompt, honest answers, one page. Start this week, not in January.
